# Fix the microphone path, then bias Parakeet

Rflow's route to much higher accuracy runs through two cheap fixes and one targeted engine change, not a bigger model. The two fixes are capture hygiene, meaning a warm WASAPI stream on a non-Bluetooth mic with pre-roll and tail, and a real measurement harness. The engine change is sherpa-onnx hotword biasing of Parakeet toward the owner's vocabulary. Deterministic phonetic correction and a confidence-gated LLM sit behind it as backstops. The evidence says the 24% vs ~6% gap is mostly not a model-quality gap. Parakeet-unified already sits within about 0.3–0.6 WER points of the best open 2B-class models on public benchmarks ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-unified-en-0.6b); [MarkTechPost 2026](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/07/23/best-open-speech-recognition-asr-models-in-2026-wer-languages-latency-and-license-compared/)). Specialised-vocabulary benchmarks show jargon WER near 90% on data where ordinary-word WER is 6–19% ([LREC 2026](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-lrec/2026.lrec-main.32/)). That pattern matches the owner's commit→clot and Claude→cloud errors. On the capture side, measurements on the owner's laptop found three problems with the current path. Opening the mic per key press costs about 350–450 ms before audio arrives. MME hides the fact that the Bluetooth earbuds deliver only 16 kHz, and in narrowband mode only 8 kHz. The default-path signal level is very low. Each of these is a plausible, fixable contributor to lost words. Several popular "improvements" measurably hurt modern ASR: off-the-shelf neural denoising, OS noise suppression and AGC, and neural bandwidth extension. The plan below therefore adopts little signal processing, measures everything, and puts vocabulary knowledge into the decoder before handing it to an LLM. The current 30-sentence test cannot reliably detect gains smaller than about 5 WER points, so Phase 0 is building an offline replay bench of 100–200+ utterances.

**How to read this report.** Statements with a citation are sourced facts from the research notes. Statements marked **(inference)** are the researchers' or this report's reasoning and have not been measured. Statements marked **[measured on owner's laptop]** come from test scripts the researchers ran on the owner's machine: Windows 11 26200, sounddevice 0.5.6, PortAudio V19.7.0-devel, built-in Intel SST array only. The Bluetooth mic was never opened. The level and spectrum figures were taken on ambient room noise, not speech. They are single or double samples, so they show direction, not precise values.

## The 24% gap points at capture and vocabulary, not at Parakeet

Three independent lines of evidence say the owner's problem is mostly upstream of the model and in vocabulary, not in raw acoustic modelling.

The first line is model quality. Parakeet-unified-en-0.6b uses a unified FastConformer encoder with an RNN-T decoder and built-in punctuation. Its card reports offline WERs of 1.63 on LibriSpeech-clean, 3.11 on LibriSpeech-other, 10.05 on GigaSpeech and 11.16 on Earnings22 ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-unified-en-0.6b)). Averaged over the eight sets, that is about **5.91% (inference, computed)**, slightly better than TDT v2's 6.05% ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v2)). The model is also noise-robust down to about 5–10 dB SNR. On v3, average WER goes from 6.34% clean to 7.12% at 10 dB and 8.23% at 5 dB, and only degrades steeply at 0 dB (11.66%) and below ([HF parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3)). Ordinary home or office noise cannot explain a fourfold WER increase.

The second line is error type. On a specialised-vocabulary dataset, WER on biased or jargon words (B-WER) was **0.88–0.90** while WER on all other words (U-WER) was **0.06–0.19** on the same audio. Putting the correct jargon into the prompt cut B-WER by 0.50–0.70 absolute ([LREC 2026](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-lrec/2026.lrec-main.32/)). A reading test dense with names and tech terms will therefore score far worse than LibriSpeech even with perfect audio **(inference)**. Part of the "24% vs 6%" is test composition, and the only way to tell how much is to split the metric into B-WER and U-WER.

The third line is the capture path, which has measurable defects **[measured on owner's laptop]**. Opening a stream costs **350–436 ms under MME and 373–379 ms under WASAPI**, and WASAPI then delivers 85–92 ms of leading zeros. Rflow opens the mic on each key press, so the first 0.4–0.6 s of speech is at risk. Mature apps treat this as the main cause of clipped first words. OpenWhispr's changelog says "Cold microphone opens could swallow the beginning of a recording; the mic is warmed" ([OpenWhispr CHANGELOG](https://github.com/OpenWhispr/openwhispr/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)). MME also reported a uniform 44.1 kHz for every input, including the Bluetooth headset. WASAPI reported the built-in array's true 48 kHz and the earbuds' true **16 kHz** **[measured on owner's laptop]**. So Rflow currently cannot see when it is recording call-quality audio. The researchers' notes say 2 of 6 of the owner's earlier recordings had no energy above 4 kHz, which is the signature of 8 kHz narrowband HFP. That observation comes from the owner's own files, not from a published source.

Narrowband audio matters for accuracy. Fricatives and aspiration live in the 4–8 kHz band. A vendor study reports 16 kHz-trained models flooring near 34% WER on 8 kHz telephone speech, which is indicative only because the setups differ ([Gnani.ai](https://gnani.ai/resources/research/how-we-train-acoustic-models-on-14-million-hours-of-telephonic-speech)). A 2026 codec study found "bandwidth preservation [is] the dominant factor" in telephony ASR ([arXiv 2606.09335](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.09335)).

Finally, ambient RMS on the default WASAPI and MME paths measured roughly **−86 to −96 dBFS** **[measured on owner's laptop]**. That is close to the 1e-5 dither floor the NeMo preprocessor was trained with ([NeMo config](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIA/NeMo/main/examples/asr/conf/fastconformer/hybrid_transducer_ctc/fastconformer_hybrid_transducer_ctc_bpe.yaml)). If the owner's speech is also very quiet on that path, quiet phonemes could sink into the floor **(inference; check peak and RMS of real recordings)**.

The notes do not determine how much of the 24% each factor accounts for. The ablations in Phase 0–1 are designed to split it.

## The target pipeline, stage by stage

The design principle behind every stage is simple. Keep the audio as close as possible to what Parakeet was trained on: clean, wideband, unprocessed and linear. Put the owner's vocabulary into decoding. Use each later stage only where the earlier stage is measurably uncertain.

### Capture: warm WASAPI at native rate, never Bluetooth by default

**Switch the host API from MME to WASAPI shared mode**, opened at the device's reported mixer rate, which is 48 kHz on the built-in array. Then resample once, in Rflow, with a known-good polyphase or sinc filter such as soxr or an exact 1:3 polyphase resampler. MME is an emulation layer on the modern stack. Its internal resampler historically defaulted to "fast integer-based linear interpolation", and on Vista-era systems it was documented to introduce audible noise ([Wikipedia: Windows legacy audio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows_legacy_audio_components)). No primary source confirms how good that resampler is on Windows 11. The stronger reason to switch is transparency: WASAPI exposes the true device rate, and that is Rflow's only cheap Bluetooth and narrowband detector **[measured on owner's laptop]**. Handing sherpa-onnx 48 kHz audio directly is also acceptable, because its internal Kaldi-style resampler is a width-6 windowed-sinc filter with its cutoff just below the target Nyquist ([sherpa-onnx offline-stream.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/master/sherpa-onnx/csrc/offline-stream.cc)). Resampling in Rflow mainly buys determinism and 16 kHz analysis for the level and bandwidth checks **(inference)**. Exclusive mode and WDM-KS bring no capture accuracy benefit and cost device sharing and robustness, so skip them **(inference)**.

**Keep a warm input stream feeding a ring buffer of about 2 s.** On key-down, prepend 300–500 ms of pre-roll. On key-up, keep recording for another 200–400 ms. This is the consensus design among the apps surveyed. Handy keeps its cpal stream always-on after open and uses a 2 s ring buffer ([Handy recorder.rs](https://github.com/cjpais/Handy/blob/main/src-tauri/src/audio_toolkit/audio/recorder.rs)), with a 450 ms VAD prefill and a 450 ms hangover ([Handy vad/mod.rs](https://github.com/cjpais/Handy/blob/main/src-tauri/src/audio_toolkit/vad/mod.rs)). OpenWhispr warms the mic with a 5 s TTL because "mic drivers go cold again after idle", and it keeps pre-roll captured from key-down ([micWarmState.js](https://github.com/OpenWhispr/openwhispr/blob/main/src/helpers/micWarmState.js); [preparedMicCapture.js](https://github.com/OpenWhispr/openwhispr/blob/main/src/helpers/preparedMicCapture.js)). Pre-opening the stream on key press does not help, because the open itself is the cost **(inference)**.

The privacy trade-off is real, because a warm stream keeps the Windows mic indicator lit. The recommended default is a warm stream after first use with an idle timeout of a few minutes, with "always-on" as an opt-in like Handy's default-off setting ([Handy docs](https://handy.computer/docs/debug)). The ring buffer must never be written to disk **(inference)**.

**Never select or keep warm a Bluetooth HFP mic by default.** Opening a Bluetooth mic switches the headset from A2DP into HFP. HFP runs at 8 kHz narrowband or 16 kHz mSBC wideband, and some wideband-capable devices fall back to narrowband with certain radios. On Windows 11 the user's playback is resampled to call quality while the mic is open ([Microsoft Learn: Bluetooth Classic Audio](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-hardware/drivers/bluetooth/bluetooth-classic-audio)). Bluetooth also adds reported activation delays of 1–2 s ([HN: Handy](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46628397)). Wispr Flow advises picking a specific wired or USB mic rather than following the system default ([Wispr docs](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/3566082841-fix-missing-first-words-in-transcriptions)).

Detection uses metadata that exists today **[measured on owner's laptop]**. The WASAPI default rate is 16 kHz or lower for HFP, and 8 kHz means narrowband. A second signal is the "bthhfenum.sys … Hands-Free" string in the WDM-KS device name. A per-recording spectral check backs this up **(inference)**: the energy fraction above about 3.8 kHz on VAD-voiced frames, plus 95% rolloff, flags narrowband even when the metadata is ambiguous. The policy is to pin the best non-Bluetooth mic, warn when Bluetooth is selected, and let the user override **(inference)**. Do not try to repair narrowband with neural bandwidth extension, which *worsened* WER in the 2026 codec study ([arXiv 2606.09335](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.09335)).

**Test which Windows processing mode to capture in, rather than assuming.** RAW mode bypasses all time-varying processing: AGC, noise suppression and echo control ([Microsoft Learn: signal processing modes](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-hardware/drivers/audio/audio-signal-processing-modes)). It fits the "unprocessed is best" evidence. On a laptop array, though, RAW also gives up the driver's beamforming **(inference)**.

The researchers confirmed that RAW and the stream category can both be set through sounddevice's hidden WASAPI stream-info struct without library changes **[measured on owner's laptop]**. RAW needs auto-convert, or else the array's native 4 channels. The Communications category failed at 1 channel. The Speech category opened at 1 channel, and its ambient noise was about **46 dB hotter** than the default category's. That strongly suggests Speech enables AGC or other processing on this Intel SST driver. Speech also added about 100 ms more leading zeros. Apps cannot query which mode the driver actually applies ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-hardware/drivers/audio/audio-signal-processing-modes)), so the only honest answer is a WER A/B across four variants: default, Speech, RAW mono via auto-convert, and RAW 4-channel with offline downmix or best-channel selection **(inference)**.

### Conditioning: measure, lightly fix, never "enhance" by default

Parakeet's preprocessor computes log-mel features, applies 0.97 pre-emphasis, and normalises each mel bin per utterance ([NeMo preprocessing docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/megatron-core/developer-guide/latest/apidocs/core/core.models.audio.nemo_audio_preprocessing.html)). sherpa-onnx supports only this per-feature normalisation ([sherpa-onnx offline-stream.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/master/sherpa-onnx/csrc/offline-stream.cc)). A constant gain therefore becomes an offset in log space and is removed. Gain matters only when speech approaches the dither and log floors, or when it clips **(inference)**.

The conditioning stage is therefore small. Remove DC first, so that metrics and headroom are honest. Optionally apply a gentle 60–80 Hz high-pass. Pre-emphasis already attenuates DC by about 30 dB, so expect no measurable WER gain from it **(inference)**. Apply a single linear peak or RMS normalisation only when the utterance is very quiet, for example RMS below about −35 dBFS or peak below about −40 dBFS **(inference; thresholds unsourced)**. Every recording should log clip ratio, peak dBFS, an SNR estimate and effective bandwidth, and Rflow should warn when these are out of range. The same metrics, collected once per device, rank the available mics automatically **(inference)**.

Do not add AGC or compression on Rflow's side. AGC amplifies background noise along with speech ([Chrome blog](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/disabling-hardware-noise-suppression/)). OpenWhispr disables echo cancellation, noise suppression and AGC on capture. Its reasons were that Chromium's AGC changes the system mic volume on Windows and that noise suppression distorts speech ([audioManager.js](https://github.com/OpenWhispr/openwhispr/blob/main/src/helpers/audioManager.js)). Keep the audio lossless all the way to the ASR. OpenWhispr measured Opus-compressed audio differing from raw in about one word in a hundred, in raw's favour ([OpenWhispr CHANGELOG](https://github.com/OpenWhispr/openwhispr/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)).

**Skip neural denoising by default.** This is the best-evidenced "don't" in the research. MetricGAN+ denoising in front of Whisper, **NVIDIA Parakeet**, Gemini Flash and Parrotlet made WER worse in **all 40** noise and model configurations, by +1.1 to +46.6 points ([arXiv 2512.17562](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17562v1)). SAM-Audio enhancement consistently raised Whisper WER ([arXiv 2603.04710](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04710v1)). The mechanism is non-linear "artifact" error, not leftover noise ([Iwamoto et al., arXiv 2201.06685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06685v2)). Blending the noisy signal back in recovers the loss. Ochiai et al. went from 16.6% enhanced to 13.0% with observation adding, against 15.9% for the noisy input ([arXiv 2404.14860](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14860v1)). Applying only 25% of FRCRN's correction took Whisper-large-v3 from 6.44% to 5.57% ([arXiv 2607.11157](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11157)).

The only defensible optional mode is a "noisy environment" toggle **(inference)**. It would run sherpa-onnx's GTCRN streaming during capture: 48K parameters, CPU RTF 0.07, and too slow to run after release ([GTCRN](https://github.com/Xiaobin-Rong/gtcrn)). It would activate only below about 5 dB estimated SNR, where Parakeet's noise curve bends, and always blend the output with the original at 0.3–0.5. Dereverberation (WPE) is linear and low-artifact, but its gains are small and come from far-field conditions ([arXiv 2102.05259](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.05259)), so treat it as an experiment only. Advise users to turn off OS or earbud noise suppression, such as Voice Focus or "clear voice" modes, because it introduces the same artifacts upstream **(inference)**.

### VAD: trim the ends, gate empty clips, keep generous padding

For push-to-talk, VAD's job is to trim leading and trailing silence and to skip ASR when there is no speech. It should not cut pauses inside the utterance. Silero VAD costs under 1 ms per 32 ms chunk ([silero-vad](https://github.com/snakers4/silero-vad)). TEN VAD runs at RTF about 0.015 ([ten-vad](https://github.com/TEN-framework/ten-vad)). sherpa-onnx ships both ([HF TEN VAD](https://huggingface.co/TEN-framework/ten-vad)). Running either during recording makes the cost after key release negligible **(inference)**.

The main risk is clipping word edges. The Silero and faster-whisper default padding is 400 ms, and users who see clipped fragments are told to raise it to 500–800 ms ([pyvideotrans VAD docs](https://pyvideotrans.com/en/vad)). OpenWhispr made VAD opt-in after it caused clipping ([OpenWhispr CHANGELOG](https://github.com/OpenWhispr/openwhispr/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)). Handy's issue #1983 reports Parakeet "sometimes miss[ing] the last word", and the report points at capture or endpoint handling ([Handy #1983](https://github.com/cjpais/Handy/issues/1983)).

There is one tension to settle by experiment. Per-utterance feature normalisation includes silence, so very long silent pads shift the statistics **(inference)**. The capture research suggests trimming to 150–250 ms of padding, while the VAD literature defaults to 400 ms. Start at about 300 ms and sweep. Two cheap guards from OpenWhispr are worth adopting: skip ASR on clips with no detected speech, and retry once when audible audio decodes to empty text. OpenWhispr's code comment reads "an empty decode of audible audio silently amputates the transcript" ([parakeetServer.js](https://github.com/OpenWhispr/openwhispr/blob/main/src/helpers/parakeetServer.js)). A Handy-style tail diagnostic, logging how many voiced frames were withheld at stop, makes missing last words visible ([Handy smoothed.rs](https://github.com/cjpais/Handy/blob/main/src-tauri/src/audio_toolkit/vad/smoothed.rs)).

### ASR plus hotword biasing: the single largest lever for names

sherpa-onnx PR #3077, merged on 5 Feb 2026, added modified beam search with hotword context biasing for NeMo transducers, including Parakeet. In its demo, "Phebe" became "Phoebe" at CPU RTF about 0.04 ([PR #3077](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/pull/3077)). The official hotwords docs still say NeMo is unsupported, and the PR supersedes them ([sherpa hotwords docs](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/hotwords/index.html)).

The researchers **verified in Rflow's own 1.13.8 install** that the Python recognizer exposes every parameter hotwords need: decoding method, max active paths, hotwords file and score, modeling unit and BPE vocab. They also confirmed that hotwords can be passed per stream, so the live "Your words" list can change without rebuilding the recognizer. The owner's earlier trial found "beam search was no better". The likely reason is that biasing silently does nothing unless all four conditions hold **(inference)**. Decoding must be modified_beam_search. The modeling unit must be "bpe", while the default is "cjkchar", which is a trap. A valid bpe.vocab must be supplied. Hotwords must actually be passed.

The practical blocker is bpe.vocab. The v2-int8 Hugging Face repo ships none, and the unified model's file list could not be checked because the repo returned 401 ([HF API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/csukuangfj/sherpa-onnx-nemo-parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v2-int8)). It can be generated from the .nemo tokenizer with sherpa's export script ([PR #3077](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/pull/3077)).

Write hotwords in the cased surface form Parakeet emits ("Claude", "Tamil", "SST") **(inference)**. Start at the default score of 1.5 and raise individual hard words to 2.0–3.0 **(inference)**. Expect false insertions if scores run high: "Claude" appearing when the owner meant "cloud" **(inference)**. Hotwords target name and term substitutions. They should not be used for ordinary-word confusions like polishing→publishing, which belong to later stages **(inference)**.

None of the surveyed open-source apps bias Parakeet decoding. Handy fuzzy-replaces after ASR, and VoiceInk and OpenWhispr lean on the LLM ([Handy transcription.rs](https://github.com/cjpais/Handy/blob/main/src-tauri/src/managers/transcription.rs); [VoiceInk AIPrompts.swift](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/blob/main/VoiceInk/Core/Enhancement/AIPrompts.swift)). Rflow would be ahead of them here. Keep greedy decoding when the vocabulary list is empty. blank_penalty mainly addresses deletions and LM/LODR fusion is heavy, so both are low priority **(inference)**. Two options are not available. The homophone replacer is Chinese-only ([sherpa homophone docs](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/homophone-replacer/index.html)). NeMo's GPU-PB and CTC-WS biasing run inside PyTorch decoding, not the exported ONNX graphs ([NeMo word boosting](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo-framework/user-guide/latest/nemotoolkit/asr/asr_customization/word_boosting.html)).

### Deterministic vocabulary correction: a zero-latency backstop

A phonetic matcher over the user's vocabulary captures most of what an LLM adds for names. In a named-entity study, phonetic replacement alone took entity WER from **32.3% to 25.3%**, against **22.7%** with an LLM. Non-entity WER stayed at about 7%, so there was little over-correction ([arXiv 2506.10779](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10779v1)). Handy's shipped version scores transcript n-grams by normalised Levenshtein distance, applies a ×0.3 Soundex boost, and uses a default threshold of 0.18 ([Handy transcription.rs](https://github.com/cjpais/Handy/blob/main/src-tauri/src/managers/transcription.rs)).

Rflow should run this after ASR with three rules **(inference)**. Auto-replace only when the match is high-precision, the source word is low-confidence, and the source is not a common dictionary word. Otherwise pass the candidate to the LLM as a hint. User "always X→Y" rules run last so they win.

Word confidence comes from per-token probabilities, which sherpa-onnx added for offline transducers ([pub.dev changelog](https://pub.dev/packages/sherpa_onnx_linux/changelog)). Whether Rflow's Python binding exposes them for this model has not been verified. Learning from the user's edits feeds this stage. VoiceInk's AutoLearn reads edits back and has an LLM review them before they become replacements ([VoiceInk AutoLearn](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/tree/main/VoiceInk/Features/Dictionary/AutoLearn)). OpenWhispr had to stop learning corrections that resolve to common English words after "why"→"what" swaps polluted its dictionary ([OpenWhispr CHANGELOG](https://github.com/OpenWhispr/openwhispr/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)).

### Confidence-gated LLM cleanup, then paste

Naive whole-transcript LLM correction is dangerous on already-good text. On Parakeet outputs from the TORGO dataset, naive correction **raised WER from 10.83% to 20.01%**. A confidence-aware corrector, given markup like `RAFELLES[0.61]`, lowered it to 10.58%. It made helpful edits 63.8% of the time on low-confidence spans versus 17.5% on high-confidence ones ([arXiv 2509.25048](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25048v1)). Routing only low-confidence utterances to the LLM avoided degrading good transcripts. The reported trade-off point was a lowest-word-confidence threshold around 0.7 ([arXiv 2407.21414](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.21414)).

Context must be selective. Feeding the full context document pushed entity WER *up* to 43.4%, while retrieving only phonetically similar, same-type entities reached 22.7% ([arXiv 2506.10779](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10779v1)). Superwhisper independently warns that long vocabulary lists hurt ([Superwhisper docs](https://superwhisper.com/docs/llms-full.txt)). Few-shot examples help. Ten demonstrations gave 21% relative gain for zero-finetune GPT-3.5 on WSJ, against 5.6% zero-shot ([HyPoradise](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.15701v1)).

The target LLM stage has five parts **(inference, synthesised from the sources above)**:

1. Call the LLM only when some word falls below the calibrated confidence threshold, or the phonetic matcher raised a candidate.
2. Send confidence markup only on the flagged words, plus "sounds like" candidates.
3. Send only the 5–20 vocabulary items retrieved for this utterance.
4. Include 3–10 few-shot pairs drawn from the owner's own past corrections.
5. Use VoiceInk-style rules: "use context only to improve transcription accuracy", and treat the transcript as content, not instructions ([AIPrompts.swift](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/blob/main/VoiceInk/Core/Enhancement/AIPrompts.swift)).

Reject any output whose word-level edit distance from the input exceeds a cap, and fall back to the deterministic text. Log every diff. Skipping the LLM for very short utterances matches VoiceInk's ≤3-word rule ([TranscriptionPipeline.swift](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/blob/main/VoiceInk/Features/Recording/Workflows/TranscriptionPipeline.swift)).

## Phased roadmap: measure first, then the cheap capture wins

The order below ranks by expected gain against effort **(inference)**. It front-loads work that is both cheap and aimed at the failure modes the evidence implicates. No phase should be declared a win without the paired statistics described in the next section.

| Phase | Work | Effort | Expected gain | Exit metric |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Phase 1a comes before hotwords even though hotwords target the dominant error class. It is cheaper, and every later measurement depends on clean, complete audio. A hotword experiment run on clipped or narrowband clips would understate the gain **(inference)**.

## Evaluation that can detect a 2-point change

The 30-sentence reading test holds about 360 words. At 24% WER, the binomial standard error is roughly **±2.3 points absolute** before accounting for correlation between utterances **(inference, computed)**. It can flag large regressions and discover vocabulary, but it cannot rank pipeline variants.

The authoritative bench should have three parts **(inference)**:

1. **Frozen test split.** 100–200+ utterances, roughly 1,000–2,000 reference words and 10–20 minutes of speech. Never used for tuning.
2. **Vocab-dev split.** Used to build hotword lists, thresholds and prompts. Vocabulary mined from a sentence must never be scored on that sentence.
3. **Real-dictation set.** Built over time from the owner's saved dictations, with corrected transcripts.

Record the test split on at least two mics and two noise conditions, and add a session on a different day. Include **contrast sentences** in which the common words that collide with vocabulary appear legitimately ("cloud storage", "a blood clot"). Without them, hotword and LLM false insertions go unmeasured.

Everything after capture replays offline against the frozen WAVs, so each toggle becomes a paired comparison on identical audio. Capture variants cannot be replayed and need their own recording sessions. For the built-in array, recording RAW 4-channel preserves the most options for deriving mono variants offline **(inference)**. Cache ASR outputs, tokens, timestamps and token probabilities so LLM variants rerun without re-decoding. Pin LLM temperature to 0 and record model versions ([post-ASR synthesis of arXiv 2407.21414 and 2506.10779](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.21414)).

Normalise both references and hypotheses with a Whisper-equivalent English normaliser. It removes fillers, expands contractions, converts numbers and maps British to American spelling ([whisper normalizers](https://github.com/openai/whisper/blob/main/whisper/normalizers/english.py)). This matters because the LLM strips fillers and Parakeet emits numerals. Without the normaliser those differences show up as fake errors. Store the normaliser version with every result **(inference)**.

Report these metrics for every configuration: WER with its substitution, deletion and insertion breakdown, CER, **B-WER and U-WER** ([LREC 2026](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-lrec/2026.lrec-main.32/)), per-term recall, false insertions of vocabulary terms, over-correction (words correct in raw ASR but wrong after correction), LLM invocation rate, and latency. Deletions at the start and end of utterances deserve their own column, because that is where capture fixes should show up **(inference)**.

For significance, compute paired ΔWER on each of 1,000 bootstrap resamples and take the 2.5–97.5 percentile CI. Resample whole sessions (blocks) rather than utterances whenever sessions differ in mic or noise. Plain utterance bootstrap underestimates variance when errors are correlated, as they are for a single speaker ([arXiv 1912.09508](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1912.09508); [BootLog](https://www-i6.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/~bisani/bootlog.html)). Call a change real only if the CI excludes zero. A sign test on per-utterance better/worse counts adds a simple second check **(inference)**.

For latency, log key release, end of capture tail, ASR done, LLM done and paste complete, and report **p50 and p95 over at least 100 dictations**, with and without LLM gating **(inference)**. Wispr Flow's published target of end-to-end under 700 ms, optimised for p99, is a useful benchmark for the gated path ([Baseten case study](https://www.baseten.co/resources/customers/wispr-flow/)). The owner reports that 3 minutes of audio currently takes about 23 s to decode (RTF ~0.13, unmeasured by the researchers). That suggests long dictations are compute-bound, and VAD-based chunking or incremental decoding would help latency more than a model change **(inference)**.

## Experiments to run on the owner's voice, and the risks they retire

Several load-bearing assumptions are untested. Each maps to one experiment on the owner's own recordings. None of these has a published answer for Parakeet specifically.

| First/last-word loss from cold open | Same passage: open-per-press vs warm plus pre-roll/tail; count start and end deletions | Warm-stream default and TTL |

Two further risks deserve naming. Hotword biasing and phonetic replacement both trade misses for false alarms. A user whose vocabulary includes "Claude" will sometimes say "cloud", so the contrast set is not optional **(inference)**. Separately, the residual gap after capture and vocabulary fixes may reflect speaker or accent mismatch with Parakeet's mostly-Granary training data ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-unified-en-0.6b)). If U-WER stays well above the 6% benchmark level after Phases 1–2, that hypothesis moves to the front **(inference)**. No front-end processing fixes it. Only a different model, or adaptation, would.

## A stronger model later changes less than the front end does

Parakeet-unified is already the most accurate English Parakeet 0.6B. It beats TDT v2 (6.05%) and v3 (about 6.3%) on averaged benchmarks ([HF v2 card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v2); [MarkTechPost 2026](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/07/23/best-open-speech-recognition-asr-models-in-2026-wer-languages-latency-and-license-compared/)). One survey note suggests v2 is the best English option, but the unified card supersedes it. The open models that clearly beat it are 1.7–2.5B LLM-decoder systems: Granite Speech 4.1 2B at 5.33%, Cohere Transcribe at 5.42%, Canary-Qwen-2.5B at 5.63% and Qwen3-ASR-1.7B at 5.76% ([MarkTechPost 2026](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/07/23/best-open-speech-recognition-asr-models-in-2026-wer-languages-latency-and-license-compared/)). The leaderboard paper confirms the pattern: transformer decoders win on WER, and CTC or TDT decoders win on speed ([arXiv 2510.06961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06961)).

That is a gain of about 0.3–0.6 points on benchmarks, with no verified x86 CPU latency figures and likely 5–20× slower decoding **(inference)**. It will not fix commit→clot on its own. What might make such models attractive is promptability. sherpa-onnx already runs Qwen3-ASR with a hotwords argument, plus Cohere Transcribe and Whisper large-v3 ([sherpa-onnx CHANGELOG](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md)). Moonshine v2 Medium (245M, 6.65%) is the small streaming alternative ([arXiv 2602.12241](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12241)). Whisper-family models add prompt echo and silence hallucination failure modes, which OpenWhispr had to engineer around ([OpenWhispr CHANGELOG](https://github.com/OpenWhispr/openwhispr/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)).

The front end recommended here is model-agnostic by design: 16 kHz float, wideband, unprocessed, end-trimmed, with no clipping and no cold-open loss **(inference)**. Every candidate model shares that input contract and benefits from it equally. The integration points that do differ between models should sit behind interfaces:

- how vocabulary reaches the decoder: transducer hotwords, a Whisper or LLM-decoder prompt, or nothing
- whether per-token confidence exists
- whether language routing is needed, for example if Tamil or Japanese dictation arrives, where Whisper, Qwen3-ASR or SenseVoice would be the candidates ([sherpa-onnx CHANGELOG](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md))

The offline replay bench is what makes a future swap cheap to judge. Each candidate gets a paired ΔWER, B-WER and p95 latency against Parakeet on the owner's own frozen audio, instead of leaderboard averages.

## Conclusion

The evidence reframes the problem. Rflow is not held back by its model but by what reaches the model and what the model is told. The researchers' measurements expose a capture path that can silently lose the first half-second, can accept call-quality audio without knowing it, and may run near the noise floor. The research literature, meanwhile, warns that the most intuitive fix, "clean up the audio", makes modern ASR worse. The highest-return moves are unglamorous plumbing (warm WASAPI, device policy, lossless audio) and a decoder feature Rflow's installed engine already supports but has not yet configured correctly (hotwords with a BPE vocabulary). LLM cleanup earns its place only as a gated, evidence-fed last resort.

The deeper implication concerns measurement. Without B-WER/U-WER separation, contrast sentences and paired block-bootstrap CIs on a few hundred utterances, Rflow cannot tell a real 3-point gain from noise, or a vocabulary win from a hotword false-alarm regression. Building that bench first costs little. It is also the asset that outlasts any particular model: it decides whether the current Parakeet, a future 2B LLM-decoder model, or an accent-adapted variant best serves this one speaker.
